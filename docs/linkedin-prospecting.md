# LinkedIn prospecting — search recipes and outreach

*Drafted 2026-09-02, for LinkedIn Premium Business. Purpose: refill the connection list with people worth inviting to follow the company page.*

## Why this exists

Page invites only go to 1st-degree connections. As of 2026-09-01 the follower base is ~58, of which 36 are in Mexico and 8 in the US — residue from promoted job posts. Every US follower came from a manual invite, converting at roughly 3 accepted per day.

So the pipeline is: **connect with the right people → invite them to follow the page → they see the posts.** Step one is the bottleneck, because a connection list built through BPO and recruiting work runs out of relevant targets fast.

## What Premium search can and cannot filter

Regular LinkedIn people search — which is what Premium Business upgrades — filters on **location, industry, current company, and keywords**. There is **no company-size filter**; that is a Sales Navigator feature. Work around it by targeting titles that only exist at the practice scale you want: an "Office Manager" is a small practice, a "VP of Revenue Cycle" is a system.

Premium also lifts the cap on personalised connection notes that free accounts hit. Use them — a request with context converts far better than a bare one.

## Search recipes

Paste the boolean into the **Title** filter, set **Industry** and **Location**, then work the results.

### 1. Practice leadership — the core target

```
("Practice Manager" OR "Practice Administrator" OR "Office Manager" OR "Practice Director")
```
**Industry:** Medical Practices; Hospitals and Health Care; Outpatient Care Centers
**Location:** Los Angeles Metropolitan Area first — see tiers below

These are the people who feel billing and credentialing pain directly and can start a conversation without committee approval.

### 2. Revenue cycle and billing

```
("Revenue Cycle Manager" OR "Revenue Cycle Director" OR "Billing Manager" OR "Director of Billing" OR "Medical Billing Manager")
```
**Industry:** Medical Practices; Hospitals and Health Care
Warmest audience for the blog and the checklists — they will recognise every problem named in them.

### 3. Credentialing

```
("Credentialing Specialist" OR "Credentialing Manager" OR "Provider Enrollment" OR "Payer Enrollment")
```
**Industry:** Hospitals and Health Care; Medical Practices
Narrow, but the credentialing checklist speaks to them precisely. High relevance per connection.

### 4. Laboratory — follow the search demand

```
("Laboratory Director" OR "Laboratory Manager" OR "Lab Director" OR "Director of Laboratory")
```
**Industry:** Hospitals and Health Care; Biotechnology Research; Medical Equipment Manufacturing
Search Console shows lab billing is the only real demand signal the site has. This audience matches the strongest asset.

### 5. Specialty practice owners

```
("Gastroenterologist" OR "Cardiologist" OR "Practice Owner" OR "Managing Partner")
```
**Industry:** Medical Practices
Slower to accept and slower to engage, but they decide. Treat as a long game, not a volume play.

## Geographic tiers

1. **Los Angeles Metropolitan Area.** Start here. The company is in Burbank, seven of eight US followers are already LA, and shared geography makes a cold connection request read as local rather than random.
2. **Rest of California** — San Diego, Orange County, Sacramento, the Bay Area.
3. **Large metros nationally** — Phoenix, Dallas, Houston, Atlanta, Chicago. Only once tiers 1 and 2 stop producing.

Do not start national. A request from a Burbank company to a Burbank practice manager needs no explanation; the same request to Atlanta does.

## Connection notes

Keep them under ~300 characters. No pitch — the aim is a connection, not a meeting.

**Practice manager, local:**
> Hi [name] — I work with independent practices around LA on billing and credentialing operations. Always useful to know other people doing this locally. Happy to connect.

**Billing / revenue cycle:**
> Hi [name] — I work on the RCM side for medical practices and follow the denial and payer-policy side closely. Would be good to connect with someone doing this day to day.

**Credentialing:**
> Hi [name] — credentialing timelines are most of what I deal with. Always interested to compare notes with people managing enrollment directly. Happy to connect.

**Laboratory:**
> Hi [name] — I work with labs and diagnostic centers on billing operations, mostly around coverage rules and denials. Would be glad to connect.

**What not to send:** anything that pitches a service, mentions a discount, or asks for a call. That converts poorly and it is the fastest way to be marked as spam.

## Weekly rhythm

- **15–20 connection requests a day** with a note. Not more — volume without relevance trains LinkedIn to treat you as spam.
- **Invite new connections to follow the page** once they accept. Budget roughly 10–12 page invites a day against the ~250 monthly credits; accepted invites refund their credit, so the real capacity is higher.
- **Check Who Viewed Your Profile weekly.** Practice managers viewing but not connecting means the profile needs work. Nobody viewing means the targeting does.

## Measuring it

With the job ads paused, the **"Organic followers" column in the LinkedIn followers export is a reliable invite-acceptance tracker** — there is no other inflow. Pull the export monthly.

The number that matters is not total followers. It is the **US share and the seniority mix** in the demographics tabs. Watch those move.

## When to consider Sales Navigator

Not yet. Two signals would change that:

1. You exhaust relevant connections and cannot find more through regular search
2. You hit LinkedIn's commercial-use search limit

Until one of those happens, Navigator buys capacity that is not the constraint.

# Filtering 6,000 connections down to the healthcare ~600

*Added 2026-09-04. The owner has ~6,000 1st-degree connections, roughly 10% healthcare — about 600 people. Page invites only reach 1st-degree connections, so this pool is warmer and should convert far better than the cold invites that ran at ~9%. Credits reset 1 October; the list should exist before then so October is spent inviting, not searching.*

## Do not invite all 6,000

Two reasons. The 250/month cap means it would take two years. More importantly, a real-estate contact who follows out of courtesy becomes a follower who never engages — and enough of those suppress reach for everyone else, which is exactly what the job-ad followers are doing now.

**Target the ~600. Skip the rest.**

## How to build the list

LinkedIn's own Connections page filters poorly. Use people search instead, which can be restricted to your own connections.

1. Search anything in the top search bar, then choose **People**
2. Set **Connections → 1st** — this restricts results to people you are already connected to
3. Add **Industry**, and separately run **Title** keywords
4. Work each search and note the names

### Searches to run

**By industry** (with Connections = 1st):
- Hospitals and Health Care
- Medical Practices
- Mental Health Care
- Outpatient Care Centers
- Hospitals

**By title** (with Connections = 1st), paste into the title filter:

```
("Practice Manager" OR "Practice Administrator" OR "Office Manager" OR "Practice Director")
```
```
("Revenue Cycle" OR "Billing Manager" OR "Director of Billing" OR "Medical Billing")
```
```
("Credentialing" OR "Provider Enrollment" OR "Payer Enrollment")
```
```
("Laboratory Director" OR "Laboratory Manager" OR "Lab Director")
```
```
("Gastroenterologist" OR "Cardiologist" OR "Physician" OR "Practice Owner" OR "Managing Partner")
```

**Borderline calls:** insurance contacts on the payer side are a maybe — they understand the problem but do not buy the service. Real estate and finance: skip, unless you know they own or invest in a practice.

## Then invite

From 1 October, page invites resume at 250/month. The invite dialog does not filter the way search does, so invite by **searching each name** from the list you built.

At 250 a month, 600 people takes about two and a half months. Expect a much better acceptance rate than the ~9% of the cold batch — these people know you.

# Thank-you notes after a connection is accepted

*The strategy is the absence of an ask.* Almost every note after a new connection is either nothing at all, "thanks for connecting!", or an immediate pitch. Being specific and wanting nothing is what makes it memorable — and it is the only version that leaves room for them to come back later.

**Rules:** two or three sentences. Name one thing that is genuinely true about their specialty's billing reality — specific enough that it could not have been sent to anyone else. No link, no calendar, no service description, no question they are obliged to answer.

## Gastroenterology

> Thanks for connecting. I work the billing side of GI — mostly the screening colonoscopies that turn diagnostic partway through, which seem to generate more payer arguments than anything else in the specialty. Not selling anything, just good to have GI people in the feed.

Alternative, if they run their own practice:

> Thanks for connecting. Most of what I see in GI comes down to whether a screening stayed a screening — Medicare wants modifier PT, commercial wants 33, and half of them get coded from one mental model. Anyway, no pitch, just glad to be connected.

## Cardiology

> Thanks for connecting. I spend most of my time on cardiology denials — the prior auth and medical necessity side, which tends to cluster into a handful of repeating patterns rather than being random. Not pitching anything, just good to have cardiology people here.

Alternative:

> Thanks for connecting. Cardiology has the most predictable denial patterns of any specialty I work with, which is either encouraging or maddening depending on the week. No agenda here, just glad to connect.

## General healthcare admin

> Thanks for connecting. I work with practices on the billing and credentialing side — mostly the parts that quietly cost money without anyone noticing. Not selling anything, just good to have more people in this world in the feed.

## What not to send

- Anything with a link, a calendar, or a "quick call"
- "Let me know if you ever need help with billing" — that is a pitch with a politeness wrapper
- A question that obliges a reply. They just accepted a connection request; making them do work is the wrong first move.

**If they reply, reply properly and still do not pitch.** The first conversation earns the second one.
